The Director

Sona Nair

Sona Nair founded Natyakala in 2017, bringing a training lineage that runs through the gurus of Kalakshetra and Kalamandalam. She began dancing at five years old, and her repertoire has since grown to include Mohiniyattam, Kuchipudi, Kathakali, Ottamthullal and Theyyam alongside her core practice in Bharatanatyam.

Now based in Minneapolis, she directs Natyakala's students with the same rigor she trained under — treating each adavu and mudra as a discipline passed hand to hand, not just performed.

Curriculum

Three classical traditions, one temple lineage.

Tamil Nadu · Temple origin

Bharatanatyam

Formalized into its present repertoire by the Tanjavur Quartet, and carried outside the temple after the 1910 performance ban was overturned.

Banned from public performance by colonial decree in 1910, Bharatanatyam was reclaimed through 20th-century protest and expanded onto secular stages worldwide — now performed as pure technique, narrative, and fusion alike.

Read more +
Kerala · Lasya style

Mohiniyattam

A solo, feminine, lasya-style dance sung in Manipravalam — Malayalam woven with Sanskrit — and rebuilt by poet Vallathol Narayana Menon.

Codified in the 18th century, ridiculed and banned under colonial rule from 1931, Mohiniyattam's revival became a point of pride for Kerala's own classical identity in the 20th century.

Read more +
Kerala · Court theatre

Kathakali

Martial choreography, elaborate mudra work, and Sanskritised Malayalam narration bring the Hindu epics to the stage in full costume.

Developed in royal courts rather than temples, Kathakali draws on South Indian martial traditions and today includes adaptations ranging from the Puranas to Shakespeare.

Why train with Sona Nair

Benefits, by age

For children

Young dancers

  • Discipline that transfers — focus, memory and posture built through adavu drills carry into school and sport.
  • Cultural grounding — students learn the stories, music and language behind each piece, not just the steps.
  • Stage confidence — every student performs; recitals are part of the curriculum from the first year.
  • A clear path — structured levels from first steps through arangetram, under one guru's eye.
For adults

Adult beginners & returners

  • Never too late — dedicated adult batches mean you learn beside peers, not children.
  • Fitness with meaning — a full-body practice of stamina, balance and grace, rooted in an art form.
  • Reconnection — for many students, a way back to a heritage they grew up around or are discovering fresh.
  • Performance optional — join the annual showcase, or simply train for the practice itself.
